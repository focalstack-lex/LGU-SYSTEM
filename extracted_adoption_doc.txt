1. Executive Summary
This document proposes the institutional adoption of the Financial Transparency and Student Academic Portal, a web-based information system developed for the College of Engineering Local Government Unit of Cor Jesu College. The system is operational and consolidates two functions that institutions typically administer through separate, manual processes: the recording, monitoring, and disclosure of organizational and student funds, and the maintenance, verification, and reporting of student academic records.
The system addresses four recurring operational gaps: financial records held in spreadsheets and paper receipts that cannot be reconciled on demand; limited visibility of fund balances for students and stakeholders; academic records maintained outside any single authoritative system; and the clerical effort required to prepare liquidation and audit documentation. These gaps are described in Section 2.
The proposed solution comprises forty-one functional modules across four domains: a financial domain covering the transaction ledger, activity budgets, receipt management, and exportable liquidation reports in Portable Document Format (PDF) and Microsoft Excel; an academic domain covering curriculum and prospectus management, credit-unit tracking, enrollment verification with program-head approval, and academic standing transcripts; a student services domain covering notifications, structured feedback, and a verifiable curriculum vitae facility with public token-based validation; and an administration domain providing the executive console, role-based access control, and an append-only audit log. The complete inventory is provided in Appendix B.
The system is supported by implemented security controls, including row-level security on all application tables, server-side token verification and role enforcement on every protected route, database triggers that prevent privilege escalation, a private receipt storage bucket with file-type and size restrictions, request rate limiting, and a weekly automated database backup with ninety-day retention. Section 4.7 states these controls precisely, including the areas identified for strengthening.
Adoption does not require the institution to develop software, but it does require a configuration workstream, because institution-specific identifiers are currently defined in the source code and the institutional database. Section 5 proposes a five-phase schedule of approximately eleven to fourteen weeks, beginning with a discovery phase and a limited pilot in one degree program. Sections 6 and 7 set out the resource requirements, an indicative cost structure, and the associated risks. Section 10 requests the designation of a focal person, approval of the pilot, and the execution of a data processing and data sharing agreement.
2. Current State and Problem Analysis
This section describes the operational conditions that the proposed system is intended to address. The conditions are drawn from the administrative circumstances the system was built to resolve and are stated in general terms so that the adopting institution may confirm which of them apply to its own operations.
2.1 Financial Records Held Across Disconnected Tools
Organizational funds are commonly tracked through a combination of spreadsheet workbooks, handwritten cash books, and physical receipt files. Each expense is recorded in the workbook by one officer, the corresponding receipt is filed separately, and the reconciliation between the two is performed only when required. Under this arrangement the record of a single transaction is distributed across at least two independent artifacts, and the accuracy of the fund balance depends on the discipline of the person performing the entry.
The practical consequence is that a balance cannot be confirmed at the moment it is requested. An enquiry received during an activity, such as whether a particular event still holds sufficient funds for a planned purchase, cannot be answered without locating the workbook and manually recomputing the affected line items. Discrepancies are discovered late, frequently at the point of liquidation, when supporting documents are already difficult to retrieve.
2.2 Limited Visibility of Funds for Students and Stakeholders
Students who contribute to collection drives, and donors who extend support, generally receive no direct view of how the funds they provided were used. Summaries may be presented at the end of a term or an academic year, but they are usually aggregate in nature and cannot be traced to individual transactions. Where disclosure is delayed, the absence of information is readily interpreted as an absence of accountability, regardless of the actual integrity of the records.
This matters most where the funds are drawn from students themselves. A collection that cannot be traced to a specific disbursement weakens the credibility of the collecting body and increases the effort required to answer questions about it.
2.3 Academic Records Maintained Outside a Single System
The verification of a student's academic load, the tracking of completed subjects against curriculum requirements, and the preparation of standing transcripts involve several independent records: the official curriculum, the student's proposed or enrolled load, the grades earned per subject, and the catalogue of prerequisites governing which subjects may be taken together. When these are maintained separately, three recurring problems arise.
First, prerequisite compliance cannot be validated reliably at the point of enrollment, and the error surfaces only when a subject is taken out of sequence. Second, a student's progress toward graduation must be reconstructed manually, which is time-consuming and prone to inconsistency between one student and the next. Third, a student who is not registered in the official roster cannot be distinguished from one who is, creating a gap in identity verification that affects every subsequent record.
2.4 Reporting, Liquidation, and Audit Preparation
Liquidation and audit preparation are assembly tasks rather than analysis tasks under a manual system. Preparing a report for a single activity requires locating every receipt associated with it, transcribing the amounts, recomputing the totals, and formatting the result for submission. The work scales with the number of transactions rather than with their value, and each report is produced independently even when the underlying data overlaps with a report already prepared.
The absence of a durable audit trail compounds the problem. When a figure is corrected in a workbook, the original entry is lost, and the person reviewing the record cannot determine what changed, when, or on whose authority.

3. Objectives and Expected Outcomes
3.1 General Objective
To establish a single, institutionally controlled system providing an accurate and auditable record of organizational funds and student academic data, available to the appropriate audience when required rather than at the end of a reporting period.
3.2 Specific Objectives
To record every transaction in a single ledger, each entry linked to its supporting receipt and attributed to its recording officer.
To compute fund balances, per-activity budget positions, and consolidated summaries automatically, so that these figures are available continuously rather than on request.
To restrict each function to the role responsible for it, so that approval authority, data entry, and read access are separated and recorded.
To maintain a permanent audit record of financial and administrative changes, including the actor's identity and the reason for any correction.
To validate each proposed load against curriculum and prerequisite rules.
To maintain a single verified student roster for account and enrollment verification.
To generate liquidation, standing, and summary reports directly from system data, in formats suitable for institutional filing and external submission.
To provide students with direct access to their own financial and academic records through a web browser, an installed mobile application, and an optional desktop application.
3.3 Expected Outcomes
The expected outcome is that routine enquiries about fund status and academic standing are answered from the system rather than reconstructed from source documents, and that the documentation required for liquidation, internal review, and audit is produced from records captured when each transaction occurred. The proposal does not assert quantified improvements in processing time or error rates, because no controlled measurement of the manual baseline exists at the adopting institution; Section 8.2 specifies the indicators and the baselines to be established before any improvement is claimed.
4. The Proposed Solution: System Description
4.1 System Overview
The Financial Transparency and Student Academic Portal is a web-based application that presents a different interface to each class of user while operating on a single shared database. It comprises a student portal, an executive console for officers and administrators, a faculty and program-head portal for load verification, a public curriculum vitae verification page, and a structured feedback facility.
The application is built as a client-server system. The interface is rendered with standard web technologies and requires no installation on the user's device. The server component handles authentication verification, role enforcement, report generation, and email dispatch. Data is stored in a managed PostgreSQL database with row-level security policies, and receipts are held in private file storage whose contents are never exposed as public links.
Two properties of the architecture are material to this proposal. First, the system is delivered as a Progressive Web Application: a student who opens it once on a mobile device may install it to the home screen, where it then opens full-screen with its own icon, without submission to any application store. Second, the system includes an optional desktop application for administrators, built on the same web interface, which provides a system tray presence and native window behaviour for office use.

4.5 Administrative Console and Role-Based Access Control
The system distinguishes eight roles. Four are operational roles within the organizational unit, namely officer, cashier, governor, and administrator. Three are academic roles, namely faculty, program head, and dean. One is the student role held by the general student body. Every protected operation is authorised on the server against the acting user's role before it is performed; the interface additionally hides actions that a role may not perform, but that concealment is a convenience and not the control.
The separation of duties is deliberate. Officers and cashiers may record transactions and manage receipts but may not create or modify activities, transfer budgets, or alter the ledger after posting. The governor may create and edit activities, post announcements, and transfer budgets. The administrator alone may assign roles, correct or delete a posted transaction, perform bulk import, and maintain the curriculum. Within the academic domain, the program head may act only on submissions belonging to the program they head, and the dean holds a read-only view of all programs. The complete role and permission matrix is provided in Appendix A.
Financial corrections follow a controlled path. A posted transaction may be edited or deleted only by an administrator, only with a recorded reason, and only in a manner that writes an entry to the audit log identifying the actor, the affected record, the reason given, and the time of the change.

Four limitations in the present security posture are disclosed for completeness, and their remediation is scheduled within the implementation roadmap. First, the content security policy permits inline scripts, which is a recognised weakening that should be removed. Second, the public transparency view is controlled by a single deployment-wide setting rather than by per-institution or per-record configuration. Third, the audit log table is present and in use but was created directly in the database and is not yet represented in the versioned schema; a consolidated baseline schema is planned so that a new deployment can be provisioned entirely from versioned files. Fourth, the feedback facility links each response to the account of the respondent; an institution intending anonymous evaluation should reconfigure this, because the current design does not provide anonymity. Section 7 treats the first and fourth items as risks with assigned mitigations, and Appendix E provides the full list of known limitations.
The institution that adopts the system becomes the controller of the personal data held within it. The system is designed to support compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and its implementing rules by providing role-restricted access, purpose limitation by module, an audit trail of access to and change of records, and a defined retention period for backups. Formal compliance, including any registration or notification obligations of the institution, remains the responsibility of the institution.

5.3 Acceptance Criteria
The pilot is considered successful, and the rollout proceeds, when all of the following have been demonstrated with the institution's own data:
Every role can perform its permitted functions and is refused the functions it is not permitted to perform, as verified by direct testing rather than by inspection of the interface.
The financial figures reported by the system for a selected period agree with the institution's source records for that period, with any difference explained and resolved.
A complete liquidation report for one activity is generated from the system, with its associated receipts, without manual assembly.
A student's proposed load is submitted, evaluated against curriculum and prerequisite rules, approved, and recorded in the cumulative record without manual transcription.
The institution's appointed administrators can provision accounts, assign roles, and locate an audit entry for a selected change without assistance.

9. Governance, Data Ownership, and Compliance
9.1 Ownership of Data and Infrastructure
All data entered into the institutional deployment is owned by the adopting institution. The institution holds the accounts for the database, the application hosting, the email service, and the domain, and becomes the sole controller and custodian of the personal data held within the system. The proponent retains no independent right of access and holds no copy of institutional data after handover.
9.2 Data Processing and Data Sharing
No personal data is transferred to the institutional environment before the execution of a written data processing and data sharing agreement between the institution and the proponent. That agreement defines the personal data to be processed, the purpose and legal basis of processing, the security measures applied, the retention period, the arrangements for deletion at the end of the arrangement, and the point at which the proponent ceases to act as a personal information processor. Where the proponent processes personal data on the institution's behalf during configuration and the pilot, it acts only on the institution's documented instructions.
9.3 Access Control and Segregation of Duties
Access is granted by role and by necessity. Administrative authority is held by a small number of named individuals designated by the institution. The recording of transactions, the approval of activities, the correction of posted entries, and the review of audit records are assigned to different roles so that no single account can complete a financial cycle without a second participant. Program heads act only within their own program. The institution may revise these assignments at any time through the administrative console.
9.4 Retention, Backup, and Deletion
Automated backups are taken weekly and retained for ninety days. The retention period for application data is set by the institution in accordance with its own records retention policy. At the conclusion of the arrangement, the institution exports the data it wishes to retain and the deployment is decommissioned, with verification that the proponent retains no residual copy.
9.5 Compliance Responsibilities
The system is designed to support compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and its implementing rules. Compliance itself remains the responsibility of the adopting institution as personal information controller. This includes determining whether registration or notification with the National Privacy Commission is required for its processing activities, appointing a Data Protection Officer where required, establishing a procedure for responding to data subject requests, and incorporating the system into its records retention, incident response, and internal audit arrangements.
9.6 Support and Escalation
During the implementation and pilot phases, the proponent provides technical support and defect correction. The support arrangement after handover, including its scope, its duration, and the point of contact on each side, is defined in the data processing and support agreement executed in Phase 0. The institution's designated administrator is the first point of contact for user enquiries, and escalates technical matters to the proponent for the duration of the agreed support period.

The Financial Transparency and Student Academic Portal is a completed, operating system that addresses a set of recurring administrative gaps with a single, auditable record. It is offered for adoption without licence fee. Appendix D: Technical Requirements and Deployment
D.1 User Requirements
Users require only a current web browser and an internet connection. No software installation, plug-in, or dedicated device is required. The interface is designed to remain usable on entry-level mobile devices and on shared or public networks, and it installs to a mobile home screen as described in Section 4.6. Permission to install the home-screen application must be available to students, since the interface is self-hosted and does not pass through an application store.

D.3 Deployment Architecture
The interface and the application server are served from a single origin over HTTPS. The server verifies authentication tokens, applies role-based authorisation, generates reports, and dispatches email. Database access is mediated entirely by the server for protected operations, with row-level security policies applied at the database level as a second layer. Receipt files are held in private storage and are served only to authenticated users with the appropriate role; they are never exposed as public links.
D.4 Provisioning and Recovery
The institutional environment is provisioned from the versioned database schema and the deployment documentation delivered with the source code. Backups are taken weekly by an automated scheduled process and retained for ninety days. Restoration is performed by applying the most recent backup to a new database instance and repointing the application server, a procedure documented in the deployment guide and rehearsed during Phase 2 so that the institution can perform it independently. As noted in Section 4.7 and Appendix E, a consolidated baseline schema is being produced during configuration so that a new environment can be provisioned entirely from versioned files; this is an acceptance item for handover.
